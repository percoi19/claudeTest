## Section 1 — Customer Personas

### Persona A — "A Camila" (The Constrained Everyday Buyer)

**Confidence: Medium** — Inferred from product context, class C/D/E profile, age range 20-35, and known behavioral patterns of underbanked Brazilian consumers. Requires validation with at least 5 direct data points before use in major messaging decisions.

**Profile**
- Age: 22-34
- Socioeconomic class: C/D
- Location: Urban periphery — Zona Leste/Sul SP, Baixada Fluminense, Salvador, BH
- Employment: Formal (CLT, lower-wage) or informal (delivery, domestic work, small vendor)
- Banking: Has an account (Caixa, Bradesco, Nubank) but no credit card — denied or never applied
- Digital fluency: High smartphone use; WhatsApp, Instagram, Shopee, iFood
- Income: R$1,400-R$2,500/month

**Primary Job to Be Done**
She needs to buy a specific item — a household appliance, a child's school supply, a phone — today, without cash to pay in full and without a credit card. She is not seeking credit in the abstract; she is solving an immediate, concrete problem.

- Functional job: Complete a specific purchase she cannot afford upfront
- Emotional job: Feel like a capable provider who handles the family's needs even when money is tight
- Social job: Not be visibly financially excluded; participate in commerce others take for granted

**Trigger Events**
- A household necessity breaks and needs replacement (washer, phone, refrigerator)
- A child needs school supplies or uniform for a new school year
- A Shopee or iFood order exceeds her current Pix balance
- She sees a time-limited deal she cannot pay for in full right now
- A friend or family member mentions they bought something in installments via Pix without a card

**Top Pains**
1. Being denied by credit cards or having a limit too low to matter — feeling locked out of normal commerce
2. High interest rates on personal loans or "carne" financing from physical stores
3. Store financing requires going in person or dealing with bureaucracy she finds intimidating
4. Her Pix balance is a fixed, often small amount — she cannot split it on her own
5. Fear around debt — she has seen family members trapped in unpayable installments

**Desired Outcomes**
- "Buy now and pay in small pieces without it ruining my month"
- Finish the purchase quickly, from her phone, without going to a physical store
- Know exactly what she will pay each month — no hidden surprises
- Keep her limit and use it again after paying

**Objections**
- "Will they approve me? I've been denied before."
- "What if I can't pay one installment — what happens to me?"
- "Is this app trustworthy? I don't want my Pix data stolen."
- "The limit of R$200-R$500 might not be enough for what I need."
- "I don't understand the fees — what am I really paying?"

**Key Vocabulary**
- "parcelar no pix" / "comprar sem cartao" / "nao tenho limite"
- "foi negado" / "carne" / "nao quero entrar em divida"
- "to sem grana" / "preciso de um credito" / "pagar em vezes" / "dar um jeito"

**How to Reach Her**
- WhatsApp (primary — she reads everything there)
- Push notifications on Android
- Instagram and Facebook ads targeting periphery zip codes
- Shopee and iFood placements (where purchase intent is highest)

### Persona B — "O Marcos" (The Rebuilder)

**Confidence: Medium** — Inferred from product context, age range 35-55, class C/D, credit-rebuilding profile.

**Profile**
- Age: 36-52
- Socioeconomic class: C/D (some D/E)
- Location: Mid-size cities and state capitals — Brasilia, Curitiba, Porto Alegre, Manaus, Recife
- Employment: Autonomous worker, micro-entrepreneur, or mid-level formal employee
- Banking: Had formal credit history — once had a card or loan — but defaulted or let it lapse. Now in credit reconstruction or resolving "nome sujo"
- Digital fluency: Moderate — comfortable with apps but cautious about new financial products
- Income: R$2,000-R$3,500/month, often irregular

**Primary Job to Be Done**
He needs access to purchasing power that his credit history has taken away. He is not reckless — he has learned from a past default — but cannot rebuild without someone giving him a first chance. He wants a controlled, low-risk re-entry into credit.

- Functional job: Make purchases for work or household management without being rejected
- Emotional job: Feel financially recovered — proof to himself that he is not "inadimplente" permanently
- Social job: Be seen (by family, partner, himself) as someone who manages money responsibly again

**Trigger Events**
- He resolves a debt negotiation and is starting fresh
- He needs to buy something for his micro-business but has no capital available
- He discovers Koin through a credit marketplace (Serasa Credito) after being denied elsewhere
- A colleague or family member tells him they got approved and made a purchase
- He receives a targeted ad while searching for financial solutions

**Top Pains**
1. Being flatly rejected by banks without explanation — the opacity of the denial is demoralizing
2. Predatory alternatives: loan sharks, "agiotas," extremely high-interest personal loans
3. Inconsistency — limit approved but then unable to use it, or reduced unexpectedly
4. Distrust of new fintech apps — burned before or knows someone who was
5. Bureaucratic friction: document requirements, selfies, waiting periods that feel like more rejection

**Desired Outcomes**
- "Get approved for something, anything, and show I can pay it on time"
- Use the product once, pay on time, and see his limit grow — tangible sign of progress
- Transparent, predictable cost per purchase — no surprises
- Be treated with dignity rather than like a risk to be managed

**Objections**
- "I've been denied by everyone — why would this be different?"
- "What's the catch? If it's easy to get approved, the fees must be brutal."
- "I can't afford to default again — the consequences aren't worth the risk."
- "Is this going to help my credit score (CPF) or is it isolated?"
- "Approving R$200 is almost an insult — that's not enough for what I need."

**Key Vocabulary**
- "nome sujo" / "score baixo" / "fui negado" / "reconstruir credito"
- "credito para negativado" / "sem consulta ao SPC/Serasa"
- "limite pequeno mas e um comeco" / "parcelar sem juros absurdos"
- "confianca" / "segunda chance"

**How to Reach Him**
- Credit marketplace platforms (Serasa Credito, Bom Pra Credito) — he is actively searching there
- Google Search ads on "parcelar pix sem cartao," "credito para negativado"
- Email (more receptive than Camila to longer-form communication)
- WhatsApp for transactional updates and limit notifications

## Section 2 — Psychology Application: Getting "Has Limit" Users to First Purchase

The conversion gap between limit approval and first purchase is almost entirely psychological, not functional. The user is eligible and capable. What blocks action is hesitation, confusion, distrust, and inertia. The five principles below address that specifically.

---

### Principle 1 — Activation Energy (BJ Fogg Behavior Model)

**Confidence: High**

**Why it applies to Koin's underbanked audience specifically**
Users in classes C/D/E have experienced so many false starts with financial products — incomplete applications, mid-process denials, confusing requirements — that they have developed conditioned hesitation. Even after approval, the question "what do I actually do now?" creates a pause that easily becomes permanent inaction. The gap between "I have a limit" and "I know exactly what to buy and how to do it" is larger for this audience than for a digitally fluent, credit-experienced user.

**Concrete implementation**
When a limit is approved, trigger a push notification and WhatsApp message that does not just say "seu limite foi aprovado." Deep-link directly into a pre-loaded purchase flow: "Seu limite de R$300 foi aprovado. Que tal parcelar sua proxima compra na Shopee? Toque aqui para comecar." Reduce steps from notification to completed transaction to three or fewer. Pre-select the most popular store (Shopee, the top merchant by volume) and show a "comprar agora" button. The goal: make the first purchase feel like it takes less effort than not buying.

---

### Principle 2 — Loss Aversion / Prospect Theory

**Confidence: High**

**Why it applies to Koin's underbanked audience specifically**
For Marcos and Camila, an approved credit limit is not routine — it is rare and felt as an achievement. Framing inaction as losing that achievement is more motivating than framing action as a gain. This audience has also experienced losing financial opportunities before (a deal they could not buy, a need they could not meet), which makes loss framing land with extra emotional weight.

**Concrete implementation**
After 72 hours without a first purchase, send a WhatsApp message: "Seu limite de R$300 esta esperando por voce — mas limites nao utilizados podem ser revisados apos 30 dias. Use agora e garanta seu credito." This is not manipulative if the policy is genuine (and it can be: unused credit lines in BNPL products are legitimately reviewed). The frame is accurate and provides a true, time-relevant reason to act. Pair it with one specific suggested purchase to eliminate decision paralysis.

---

### Principle 3 — Social Proof / Bandwagon Effect

**Confidence: Medium** — Mechanism is sound; specific effectiveness for this Brazilian segment on a financial product needs testing.

**Why it applies to Koin's underbanked audience specifically**
This audience is heavily word-of-mouth driven. Trust in financial products is low by default — Brazilian classes C/D/E have been targeted by scams, predatory lenders, and failed fintechs. Social proof from "people like me" (peers, not celebrities) is the fastest available trust accelerator. When Camila hears "minha vizinha usou e funcionou," that is worth more than any ad. Koin needs to bring that word-of-mouth dynamic into the activation flow.

**Concrete implementation**
On the post-approval screen, display a real counter: "Mais de 47.000 pessoas fizeram sua primeira compra este mes." Below it, add 2-3 short testimonial snippets from real users in similar demographics: "Comprei meu celular novo na Shopee em 4x sem cartao — Fernanda, SP" and "Parcelei o forno que precisava urgente — Ricardo, BA." These must be real, not fabricated. Where data allows, add a localization signal: "pessoas da sua regiao tambem usaram Koin."

---

### Principle 4 — Hyperbolic Discounting / Present Bias

**Confidence: High**

**Why it applies to Koin's underbanked audience specifically**
Present bias is especially pronounced in lower-income populations where immediate needs are more pressing and future-oriented financial thinking is a luxury of stability. Camila and Marcos are not failing to act because they do not value the limit — they are failing because the need is abstract until it is urgent. A vague future benefit ("you can buy things someday") will always lose to an immediate concrete need. Koin must collapse the time between "limit approved" and "specific thing I want to buy right now."

**Concrete implementation**
Redesign the approval journey to end with a purchase, not a confirmation screen. After "parabens, seu limite foi aprovado," continue with: "O que voce quer comprar primeiro?" Present three pre-selected options based on time of day and top merchants: "Pedir no iFood agora," "Comprar na Shopee," "Usar um codigo Pix de qualquer loja." Frame each with an immediate benefit: "Peca jantar hoje e pague em 3x." The user has momentum and intent in this exact moment — do not waste it by sending them to a home screen.

---

### Principle 5 — Commitment and Consistency

**Confidence: Medium** — Mechanism is well-established; specific application to post-approval BNPL flows needs product-level testing.

**Why it applies to Koin's underbanked audience specifically**
Both personas went through a meaningful registration and credit evaluation process — they gave data, took a selfie, waited for a result. That process is itself a commitment signal. Koin can leverage the consistency principle: "Voce completou o cadastro. O proximo passo natural e fazer sua primeira compra." This frames the first purchase not as a new risky decision but as the natural completion of something already started. It is especially effective for Marcos, who is motivated by demonstrating to himself that he follows through.

**Concrete implementation**
In a Day 3-5 push notification (for users with an approved limit but no purchase), use completion framing: "Voce esta a 1 passo de ativar sua conta Koin. Faca sua primeira compra e seu limite comeca a crescer." Add a progress indicator on the app home screen: a two-step visual showing "Limite aprovado [check]" -> "Primeira compra [pending]." This secondarily triggers the Zeigarnik Effect — the unfinished task creates cognitive tension that pulls the user toward completion.

## Section 3 — Referral Program Design

**Note on LTV and CAC:** Exact figures are not available in the context files. Design below assumes CAC of R$25-R$60 (typical for Brazilian BNPL performance campaigns) and LTV of R$150-R$400 over the first 6-12 months. These assumptions must be validated before launch. **Confidence: Low on sizing specifics; Medium on structural design.**

---

### Trigger Moment

The optimal trigger is immediately after the first purchase is completed — not at registration, not at limit approval. This is the peak of the Peak-End Rule: the user just successfully bought something they could not buy before, without a credit card, using Pix. The emotional state at this moment (relief, satisfaction, "isso funcionou") is the highest-motivation context for a share request.

Secondary triggers:
- After a successful repayment when the limit is restored ("it keeps working")
- After a limit increase ("I proved myself and the limit went up")

Never ask for a referral during the limit approval wait or from a user who has not yet made a purchase. Asking before delivering value destroys trust with this audience.

---

### Share Mechanism

Ranked by recommended priority for Koin's audience:

1. **WhatsApp share (primary)** — This audience lives on WhatsApp. A single-tap "compartilhar no WhatsApp" button that pre-populates a message with the user's personal referral link. The message must sound natural and peer-to-peer, not corporate: "Ei, to usando o Koin pra parcelar no Pix sem cartao. Se voce nao tem cartao e quer parcelar, baixa pelo meu link — voce ganha [incentivo] na primeira compra: [link]"

2. **Personalized referral link** — For users who want to share via Instagram stories, SMS, Facebook. Tied to user's CPF or account ID for attribution.

3. **Referral code** — For offline/verbal sharing. Six-character alphanumeric codes tied to the account. Simple enough to remember and share in a conversation.

Social media broadcast sharing (public Instagram, Facebook posts) is lower priority for this audience — share behavior is personal and direct-message-driven, not public broadcast.

---

### Incentive Structure — Double-Sided Reward Recommendation

**Recommended: Double-sided, cash credit added directly to the user's Koin limit**

Given the audience's profile (cash-constrained, credit-motivated), the highest-value reward is direct credit added to their Koin limit — not gift cards, not points, not discount coupons. Real purchasing power is the most legible and motivating reward for Camila and Marcos.

**Referrer reward (existing user):** R$20-R$30 credit added to their Koin limit when the referred friend completes their first purchase. This directly links sharing to more buying power — the exact thing they care most about.

**Referred friend reward (new user):** R$20 credit bonus added on top of their approved limit after their first purchase. An approved limit of R$200 effectively becomes R$220. This directly addresses the "the limit is too small" objection.

**Rationale:**
- Credit-to-limit is the most valuable reward form for this audience — it enables more of what they came for
- Double-sided framing creates a "gift" narrative ("I am giving you a real benefit, not just helping myself"), making the share feel generous rather than transactional
- Reward only triggers after first purchase — filters for genuine users, not registration farmers
- Sizing at R$20-R$30 is roughly 6-15% of initial limit: meaningful but not so large it creates fraud arbitrage incentives

**Second-order consideration:** Rewarding only on first purchase trains users to value the program for its genuine utility, not to game it. Volume of referral shares will be lower than programs rewarding on registration, but quality of referred users will be higher. This tradeoff is correct for Koin: a 65% limit-denial rate means low-quality referred users waste the credit evaluation step and add cost without revenue.

---

### Fraud Prevention Considerations

**Confidence: Medium** — Standard BNPL referral fraud patterns; Koin-specific rates unknown.

1. **Self-referral / device farming:** Users creating multiple CPF accounts to refer themselves. Control: one reward per referred CPF, device fingerprinting at registration, flag multiple activations from same IP/device cluster within 30 days.

2. **Fake registration farms:** Coordinated sign-ups using purchased CPF data. Control: reward releases only after credit evaluation AND first purchase — do not weaken this trigger under any circumstances. Velocity rules: flag any referrer with more than 10 referrals in 30 days for manual review.

3. **Evaluation bypass attempts:** Referred users attempting to use referral status to influence credit scoring. Control: referral status must have zero effect on credit evaluation. The incentive is post-approval credit, never approval itself.

4. **Reward harvesting without genuine use:** Users who refer, collect the credit, and go dormant. Control: the credit-to-limit reward is only useful if the user continues buying. There is no cash-out mechanism. This structure naturally filters for genuine users.

5. **Program cap:** Maximum of 10 referral rewards per user per 90-day period (R$200-R$300 total). Communicate this clearly at program launch.

---

### 3 Most Important KPIs

1. **Referral Activation Rate** — percentage of referred users who complete their first purchase, compared to the baseline activation rate of non-referred users. This is the primary quality signal for the program. Target: referred users activate at least 15 percentage points above the overall baseline.

2. **% of New Activated Users from Referral** — of all users who complete a first purchase in a given month, what proportion came through a referral link. Measures the program's contribution to the growth engine. Target in steady state (month 3+): 10-20% of new activations attributable to referral.

3. **Referral CAC vs. Paid CAC** — total cost of rewards paid out divided by referred users who activate, compared to paid media CAC from Meta, Google, and credit marketplaces. If referral CAC is below paid CAC with comparable or better LTV, the program justifies scaling. This is the economic sustainability signal.

## Section 4 — Research Gap Analysis

These five gaps represent the highest-leverage unknowns — the ones that, if answered, would most change Koin's marketing decisions on messaging, channel allocation, activation tactics, and referral design.

---

### Gap 1 — Why do "has limit" users not make their first purchase?

**Confidence this is a gap: High**

The funnel data shows ~35% of registered users receive a limit, and first purchase is the activation event. But what percentage of users with an approved limit actually buy — and why don't the rest? Is the drop-off because: (a) the limit is too small for the desired purchase, (b) users do not understand what to do next, (c) they do not trust the app enough to proceed, (d) the UX is confusing, or (e) life pulled them away? This is the most important conversion gap in the entire funnel and the answer changes almost every activation tactic.

**How to find out:** Exit survey triggered 7 days after limit approval for users who have not purchased. Single question in WhatsApp or in-app: "Voce ainda nao fez sua primeira compra no Koin. O que esta impedindo?" with five answer options plus an open text field. Target at least 200 responses before drawing conclusions.

---

### Gap 2 — What purchase need drove the user to download Koin in the first place?

**Confidence this is a gap: High**

Top merchants by volume are known, but it is not documented which drives the most first purchases, what product categories are most common, or whether users arrive with a specific item in mind (bottom-of-funnel intent) versus browsing for credit options (top-of-funnel). This distinction fundamentally changes the post-approval journey: a user who already knows what they want to buy needs a fast path to checkout; a user who does not yet have a specific item needs a curated browsing experience.

**How to find out:** Analyze existing transaction data for first purchases by merchant, product category, purchase value, and time between limit approval and transaction. This data likely already exists internally. If not, add a single post-first-purchase question: "O que voce comprou na sua primeira vez com Koin?"

---

### Gap 3 — What is the emotional experience of the denial moment, and what do denied users do next?

**Confidence this is a gap: High**

65% of registered users are denied a limit. There is no active nurturing strategy for this segment. But understanding how denied users feel (humiliated, resigned, hopeful, angry) and what they do next (delete the app, wait, try a competitor, tell others) would determine: (a) whether a nurturing program is worth building, (b) how to communicate the denial without destroying future intent, and (c) whether denied users are a referral source for friends who might qualify.

**How to find out:** App Store review mining — search for "negado," "nao aprovei," "limite zero" in 1-3 star reviews. Analyze support tickets segmented by users with no approved limit. Consider a single WhatsApp message to denied users 30 days post-denial: "Ainda nao conseguiu seu limite? Conte pra gente o que aconteceu" with a short open-text response option.

---

### Gap 4 — What is the primary trust signal that moves a skeptical user from "I downloaded the app" to "I trust this enough to give my data and buy"?

**Confidence this is a gap: Medium**

This audience has legitimate reasons to distrust financial apps. But the specific trust signals that work for this segment — is it social proof from peers, Banco Central authorization logos, brand familiarity from known stores, interface quality, or something else — are not documented. Knowing the primary trust accelerator would reshape the onboarding flow, the store selection on the home screen, and paid media creative (which currently has unknown messaging angles).

**How to find out:** Reclame Aqui reviews for Koin and direct competitors (Pagaleve, Jeitto, Neon Parcelado) — tag verbatims by what users cite as reasons they did and did not trust the app. r/financaspessoais and r/brasil on Reddit. App Store 3-4 star reviews (these are the most analytically valuable: users who almost liked it but had a reservation). Code themes by frequency and intensity.

---

### Gap 5 — How does word-of-mouth actually work in this audience — who do they tell, what do they say, and when?

**Confidence this is a gap: Medium**

The referral program design above assumes WhatsApp peer sharing as the dominant channel and that both personas are embedded in networks of people with similar credit profiles. But the actual mechanics are unknown: do people openly discuss financial products with friends? Who are the "credit advisors" in their social network (a family matriarch, a WhatsApp group admin, a trusted coworker)? What do they say when recommending — or warning against — a product? This knowledge shapes not just referral program design but the full organic acquisition strategy.

**How to find out:** Qualitative interviews with 10-15 users who have made at least 2 purchases. Conduct via WhatsApp voice message exchange to minimize friction. Key questions: "Voce ja falou com alguem sobre o Koin? O que voce disse?" and "Se voce fosse recomendar pra alguem, como voce explicaria o Koin em uma frase?" These 10-15 interviews are the highest-ROI research investment available to the team right now. The language captured in these conversations feeds directly into referral program messaging, push notification copy, and paid media creative.
